What gets measured

Ideal values are calibrated to MediaPipe's averaged face mesh (averageness itself predicts attractiveness, Langlois & Roggman 1990), then shifted where studies show attractive faces differ from average. Measurements explain part of attractiveness, not all of it; treat the rating as a guide.

Beyond the face

  • Body fat. Facial adiposity is one of the strongest cues people read (Coetzee et al. 2009). Getting lean sharpens the jaw, cheekbones and eye area more than any trick.
  • Posture. Forward head posture hides the jaw and adds a double chin. Chin tucks, upper-back rows, and a screen at eye level.
  • Teeth. White, aligned teeth raise smile ratings. Whitening strips, retainer or aligners if crowded.
  • Sleep and water. Sleep-deprived faces are rated less attractive and less healthy (Axelsson et al. 2010).
  • Style. Clothes that fit your shoulders and waist, colors that contrast your skin and hair, a haircut every 4 to 6 weeks.
  • Skip. Bone smashing, DIY fillers, unverified pills and tanning beds. They cause damage that no rating is worth.
